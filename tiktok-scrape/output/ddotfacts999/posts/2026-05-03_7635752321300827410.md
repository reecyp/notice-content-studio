## Caption

Mindfulness is the practice of purposely focusing attention on the present moment—thoughts, feelings, and bodily sensations—without judgment to reduce stress and improve mental clarity. Beginners can start by taking 5–10 minutes daily to focus on their breath, practicing mindful eating, or using the "STOP" technique (Stop, Take a breath, Observe, Proceed). [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] Key Tips for Beginners Start Small: Begin with just 1–5 minutes of focused breathing, focusing on the tip of the nose, chest, or abdomen.Be Kind to Your Mind: It is normal for the mind to wander. When you notice, gently return your focus to your breath without frustration.Find a Comfortable Seat: Sit in a comfortable position, such as on a chair or cushion, with a straight back and relaxed shoulders.Daily Routine Integration: Choose one routine activity (e.g., brushing teeth, drinking coffee) to perform with full awareness of your senses. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7] Simple Mindfulness Exercises The STOP Technique: When feeling overwhelmed, stop what you are doing, take a breath, observe your sensations/thoughts, and proceed with awareness.Mindful Eating: Pay close attention to the texture, smell, and taste of your food while eating without distractions.Body Scan: Mentally scan your body from head to toe, noticing sensations without trying to change them. [1, 2, 3] Common Misconceptions It’s Not About Clearing Your Mind: Mindfulness is not about emptying your head, but rather noticing where your attention goes without judgment.There’s No "Right" Way: If you struggle to stay focused, you are still meditating. The act of noticing your mind wander is mindfulness. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5] Benefits of Mindfulness Consistent practice can reduce cortisol (stress hormone), lower blood pressure, improve sleep quality, and increase focus and self-compassion.
